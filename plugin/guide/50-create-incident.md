## How to create an incident

### Fill in the call

1. Press the green **+ New**, to the right of the tabs.
2. Under **Incident Type**, pick the kind of call. The first type in the list is already picked, so check it. Your agency’s own types may have a short code in front, such as **MVA - Traffic Accident**.
3. Set the **Location**. See [Set the location](#set-the-location) below.
4. Under **Details**, type what is happening.
5. Press **Caller Info**, near the bottom of the form, to open it.
6. Type the caller’s name and phone number.
7. Check the type and the details. You can’t change them after the next step: later you can only add notes. The location can still be changed later (see [Change the location](60-work-the-call.md#change-the-location)).
8. Press **Create Incident** at the bottom of the form.

The new incident opens, with the next number, ready for units, notes and more callers (see [How to work the call](60-work-the-call.md)). **← Back** goes to the list. Press **Show on Map**, at the bottom of the incident, to see where it is.

In the list, each incident is a short card: its number and the time it was created, the type, the address, and the units on it. A new incident is under **Active** (in Multi-Monitor Mode, under **Unassigned**). Press a card to open that incident.

If a yellow line says the incident **was created, but** something didn’t go out, field units may not have it on their maps. Tell them by radio.

> [!TIP]
> - Leave **Incident Time** as it is. The incident gets the time you press **Create Incident**, even if you change this box.
> - **Report Number** can wait. You can add it later (see [Add a caller or a report number](60-work-the-call.md#add-a-caller-or-a-report-number)).
> - Employee numbers: the **Taken by #** box shows your number. Change it only if you took the call for someone else.

### Set the location

Every incident needs a spot on the map. Use one of these three ways.

#### A. Search for the address or the place

1. Under **Location**, type in the **Search address…** box: an address, two cross streets (such as `Main & 1st`), or the name of a place.
2. Wait a moment, or press **Search**. The list starts after about four letters.
3. Press the right line in the list. Or press the down arrow key to move to it, then **Enter**.

The address boxes and the map spot fill in. Each line in the list has a tag that says where it comes from:

| Tag | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| **Place** | A place from your agency’s loaded list, such as a school or a park. Its name also goes on the first line of **Details**. |
| **Verified** | Your agency’s own address data. Trust these first. |
| **OpenStreetMap** | The public map. It needs internet. Always confirm it is the correct and intended address. |

If you only see **OpenStreetMap** lines, your agency’s data has no match for what you typed, or none is loaded.

If the address has units, such as apartments, up to six show as buttons next to **Unit:**, under the search box. Press one, or type the unit in the **Unit** box, beside the street address.

#### B. Drop a pin on the map

1. Press the pin button (📍) next to **Search**.
2. Click the spot on the map.

The **Latitude** and **Longitude** boxes fill in, and the nearest address fills the address boxes. A line under the search box says which address it took and how far it is **from your click**. Check it. If it is the wrong one, press one of the other addresses listed under it. The spot stays where you clicked. If the line says **No address found near that point**, type the address yourself (way C).

#### C. Nothing found: type the address yourself

1. Drop a pin on the spot first (way B).
2. Type the street address, city and state in the boxes.

Keep this order. Dropping a pin can replace an address you already typed.

> [!TIP]
> **No internet?** The search then finds only your agency’s own data, if it was loaded. No message shows: the list is just short or empty. Drop a pin and type the address yourself (way C). Do the same if a line says **Address search isn’t available. Drop a pin and type the address.**

> [!TIP]
> **“Coordinates are required”** means the incident has no spot on the map yet. Drop a pin, then press **Create Incident** again.

### What not to type

- Near the top of every incident is a yellow CJIS reminder. Follow it: don’t put CJIS information in an incident.
- Field units see what you type in **Details** and in notes on their map. They also see caller names and phone numbers, unless **Caller Details** is turned off in **Dispatcher Settings**. Type only what field units should see.
